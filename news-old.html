2016.05.20
Approaching our 2 year anniversarry, let's talk about osu! mapping!

It has been a while, but hello! Not really sure how many people will decide to read this, but at least I'm writing something.
So, on June 20th, 2014 - this website launched for the first time. Over its 2 years, it's seen lots of drastic changes. It used to have an ugly as hell interface, there was generally lots of clutter, et cetera. There were lots of things wrong with it. Now, I'd personally say the website looks sleak, though it's still a huge mess. But I guess I don't really have to worry, since no one really comes here a lot.
Anyway, despite it being almost 2 years since the website had launched, I don't really have much planned. Mainly a few more website clean-ups and I'll try to post here more frequently about things I want to talk about, such as this post.

So, let's talk about mapping in osu!
First of all, to any new visitors who've never heard of osu! - osu! is a free rhythm game basically that can be downloaded here - http://osu.ppy.sh/
Here's a gameplay video of the game's main game mode. The game also has 3 other game modes, being Taiko, osu!mania & Catch the Beat.
That game has an editor where you can take a song and make a beatmap out of the song itself for others to play, which is gonna be the focus of today's post.

Regardless...I first started mapping in osu! at around June 2015. I submitted my first map - Woody Alien - Don't Stop (later found out it was called Alchemist Project - Don't Stop, so it was later renamed). I had no prior knowledge of using the editor or anything about mapping whatsoever, so the map ended up being rather terrible, with repetitive cicles and sliders being rarer than blue moons. Back then, I had no idea what even Kiai time was, or timing points! I even had trouble setting up the offset & BPM! (well, I still do, but not on as huge of a margin as back then!) If you're trying to imagine how it used to play back then, then, like I said, - imagine a 3 minute 120BPM map where you only have to click circles in a 1/4 beat with no patterns & some of the timings being awfully off.
When I received criticism for the map, I took it very negatively. Trying something new really discourages you from continuing it when you start to realize how much time it takes and how wrong you end up doing it and I didn't end up touching the editor much after.

At around August 2015, something clicked inside of me. It was about...a month since I have become a full on Hyperdimension Neptunia fan and back then, only recently started to play the 3rd game in the series, named Hyperdimension Neptunia Victory for PS3. That game's opening sequence, named "Kami Jigen! Fortune Material" - captivated me - catchy, great vocals, great visuals, basically - I loved it. I wanted to find a beatmap of the song on osu!, and to my surprise - I ended up finding one, however, let's say that it wasn't good. At all. There were a lot of mistimed sliders, circles, the offset is set completely wrong, the BPM is miscalculated - basically, it sucked. At the time, I thought to myself "I bet I can do a lot better than that", despite only having 1 map under my belt at the time, I still went with it. And thus, my very own version of nao's "Kami Jigen! Fortune Material" was born. When I initially released it, it had only 1 difficulty - Light Insane (now named Hard). And most of my friends who played Don't Stop were shocked to see how much better my Light Insane difficulty on Kami Jigen was than Don't Stop's difficulty! And getting this praise motivated me to continue mapping.
With this map - I learned what Kiai time was for the first time. I learned of timing points, of different BPM settings. of the most basic hitsounding - I learned a lot! I still ended up asking my friends, who were more experienced mappers, for advice on BPM & Offset, but through all the critique I've received on Kami Jigen's difficulty, I really ended up learning a lot and to this day - I am thankful to not only all the people who helped me back then with that map, but also to myself for not giving up.
With this new motivation, I ended up making 2 new difficulties - Normal & VICTORY. First came Normal. It was my first ever attempt at something 1 or 2 stars and I thought that I could use the same things I learned from Don't Stop & the previous Light Insane difficulty to make this Normal difficulty, however, I've learned that there are more strict guidelines one must follow for Normal-esque difficulties and the Normal difficulty ended up being a total disaster, with the difficulty being officially removed from the map on March 3rd, 2016. After the difficulty's removal, I consulted with one of the guest difficulty owners for him to possibly remap his "Easy" difficulty into a "Normal" one, with it becoming "mcwc2307's Normal" the following day and has stayed like that since. And I haven't made a "Normal" type difficulty since the removal of the Normal difficulty from Kami Jigen either.
VICTORY is a different story. Originally, I set out the map to be a 5 star map, with me trying to include as many streams & jumps as I possibly could, with this difficulty also being my first attempt at making something truly difficult. There were only a few rules. No literally impossible jumps or streams. Everything else was a basic free-for-all and for having the experience I did back then - I say I did an okay job at the time. However, when I had finished mapping it, I was disappointed when I found out that I had only made the map 4,93 stars at the time. I wanted it to be at least 5,10! So I made the worst possible thing any inexperienced mapper could do - I reduced it to CS6 (small), from CS4 (normal) (CS being Circle Size, not Counter-Strike for those wondering...). And so, the map became 5,44 stars. Finally! The map is at the point where I want it to be! I was happy. But players were not. I remember getting PM'd by various modders to change the CS back down to 4 or something among the lines of 4.5. I wildly refused, calling anyone who couldn't play it a "total noob". But after playing through the map myself, I decided to change it back down to around CS4, since even I had trouble playing the map.
After that, a swarm of mods came in, and eventually - the difficulty ended up becoming 4,45 stars - almost 1 entire star lower than its peak before.
Since March 6th, 2016 - I have completely ceased to update the map itself. Not even responding to the guest difficulty owners asking me to update their versions on my mapset, with me telling them to submit it to their own mapsets if they like.
What's next for this map is basically nothing. I do not plan to update it anymore and it'll just sit there as a sort of memorial. A memorial of my second ever osu! beatmap - the one I've worked on THE MOST out of all the beatmaps I've ever made, yet it ends up being one of the worst I've made, at least by popular playtester opinion.
What I owe the map, though, is a complete re-map. Possibly this summer, I plan to remap Kami Jigen, at least the VICTORY difficulty, and truly showcase what I can do today, but for now - let's move on.

September 2015. About 2 weeks after my PC had died. I encounter some Neptunia meme, named "REMOVE EGGPLANT", which is a parody on the "REMOVE KEBAB" meme. It was a short 20 second song, so I decided to map it and thus - REMOVE EGGPLANT by me was born. However, the map didn't last long. It was supposed to be a troll map, not to be taken seriously and I didn't really end up putting much effort into it. The map was officially trashed on December 2015, so it's no longer available, except as a mirror on my osu! profile.

​The next 3 maps don't really have an intricate story to tell, so I'll keep this one brief. I made a few more maps after REMOVE EGGPLANT, being Nine Circles (Short Ver.), a "Hard" guest difficulty for a friend's map on nao's "Symmetric Generation" song and a beatmap of Helblinde's old song named "Ritsuen". Not much effort was put into these maps, most of them being deleted, apart from the guest difficulty. Out of these, I'd definitely say that Ritsuen was my best map, but even then - that map had some...questionable things in it. Overall - I didn't end up learning much from these, other than a "Convert Slider to Stream" feature in Ritsuen, which, while useful, I didn't end up using a lot in the future.

But as for my next map...let's say I tried a bit too hard on this one. Around January 2016, I ended up joining some sort of small anime community, which had an "Internet Radio" feature in it. Through that community - I was introduced to the vocaloid world outside of Hatsune Miku for the first time ever and had a listen to some really great vocaloid songs, one of them being a remix of CREATIVE from Don't Hug Me, I'm Scared. I loved it. The song was incredible to me. And just like with Kami Jigen! Fortune Material, I tried searching up a beatmap of the song to play. However, this time - I found nothing. I'm serious, this song had no beatmap of it! And this was my chance. No one made a beatmap out of this song yet. I could be the first one! And so, I opened the editor, slapped the song in and begun work. From 8 AM to around 3 PM - I mapped without break. Cicle after circle, slider after slider, spinner after spinner. And finally - it was finalized & released! While the mapping quality isn't really on-par with what I can do today, I am overall proud of what I had done in that map. Open it up in the editor and you can really see that I worked A LOT on that map. Hitsounding being the main focus here. Yes, I was practicing mainly hitsounding with this song - what types of sounds to use, when to use them, etc. and overall - I learned much about hitsounding from that map. I am planning a bit of an improvement to the map, because I really feel as if the current version isn't that...good. But yeah - being the first one to map the song & learning in the process makes me feel good about it to this very day. My friends like to describe the map as "awful, yet fun", which is a trend that continued over the rest of my maps after CREATIVE.

Now we're getting to the so-called "Modern Era". This is basically the maps I made nowadays & still support.
First one being "furioso melodia" by gmtn. (witch's slave). The amount of hours I've spent working on this map is unbelievable for me personally. Originally, I spent about 17 hours mapping the very first version of the map ever, with an additional 11 days of waiting to submit the map. But those 11 days of waiting were the most challenging parts of the entire process for me personally, not because I had to play the waiting game - but because people wanted to mod my map while it was still unsubmitted. The amount of negative backlash I received at the time was honestly heartbreaking. "your timings fucking suck" "none of this shit matches up" "do you even fucking know that this song is 1/3 and 1/6 and not 1/4? fucking retard" was very very common. No one seemed like they wanted to help me fix issues on the map, everyone I asked to playtest & mod just dismissed my map as horrible. And I was planning to not submit the map entirely at one point. However, one guy was actually willing to not only help me with the map & fix basically everything, but he also decided to teach me how to map in 1/3 and 1/6. I really wish I could remember the guy's username, but he was such an angel about this, it's unbelievable. After all that negative backlash & everyone hating the map in its original state, this one guy decided to be nice & helpful to me...anyway, he taught me 1/3 & 1/6, told me where exactly I should use 1/3 & 1/6, and with his advice - my map magically started to fit timings almost perfectly! I was happy, really! I started to get motivation & hope back for my furioso melodia map, I sent out the map to the same people who called it bad and they started to change their opinions, calling it "not as bad tbh". All of this was around 2 days before I could submit the map, due to the beatmap submission limit of 3 active beatmaps at once.
Once I could submit the map - I did, alongside a Reddit post which actually ended up being successful, with the community providing an unmeasurable amount of useful feedback that wasn't "wow this fucking sucks"! (expect for 1 guy).
With each day passing, I keep thinking of new ways I could improve my furioso melodia map. What kind of patterns I could try, how should I set my timing and I still do this to this very day!
This map made me lose motivation, gain it back, then lose it again and then gain it back!
The map taught me 1/3 and 1/6, the map gave me ideas for other maps - the map showed me that the osu! community can be helpful, if the right time & place is picked.
I love my furioso melodia map, and with it having 20 favorites, as of writing this post, it's my most successful map I've ever made on osu!
I really hope I get to keep improving the map, maybe even one day I could rank it, haha.

And now - the last map. Chipzel - Waiting.
I don't really have much to say about this one. I mapped it for fun in under a day and I just wanted to put my skills to the test. My friends call it a fun map and it's by far the hardest map I've ever made without resorting to artificial methods, such as decreasing the circle size.

Overall - what's the point of this entire post?
It's a message to newbie osu! mappers, from another newbie mapper - don't be afraid, don't lose motivation. Receiving criticism on your creations is harsh & painful, I know. I've lived through the worst with my few first maps & my furioso melodia map. But don't give up. Your map can be made great. If you feel like it can't - start fresh. Abandon your old map if you have to and map a new song.
The more you map - the more you learn!

I hope you found this relatively interesting.
If any of you want to try my deleted maps (ones that I didn't link in this post) - you can do so by going to my osu! profile here, scrolling my userpage down a bit to the "My maps" section.
Reading wasn't your thing or it didn't give enough information as you liked? Then just play the maps themselves and have a look for yourself on how much I've progressed.

Thanks for reading, see you in a few months with my next post!
​-Site Owner

2014.11.28
Issues with the updates

Hey guys.
So...while play-testing the new level_float_set commands on SH2-TUS, I kinda run into some issues.
First of all - removing increment = removing octagon and pentagon modes.
Since getting into those modes was solely based on increment time, removing the increment would equals the same as removing the two modes in general. And since I don't like it when my packs feel too dull, removing them would pretty much sin the entire pack.

Next up is the level speeds. Rotation increases fine, however - the polygons don't.
It causes some really fucked up shit to appear, like completely closed off spirals, many different walls coming at you at various speeds and other stuff.
This kinda makes the transition from one level to another a really hard one. You basically have to pray you don't get a closed off wall at you.

And finally - inconsistency.
Some levels apply the level_float_set command just fine, while others completely ignore it.
I am speaking specifically of the two levels I always overlook during development - Bright Pirate and Crazy Pirate.
I just can not get level_float_set to work on those levels.
This just signals for an entire level remake. Ugh...

Until I find a remedy for all these situations, the update on SH2-TUS is gonna be layed off.
Boson X for Open Hexagon will be updated to this new modern form soon though, unsure if over the weekend or not.
And Rotating Madness won't even receive this update, simply because I don't really wanna bother with it anymore and doesn't really need it as far as I am concerned. All RM needs is an update on "Waiting..." because the increment time is way off.

It's always fun to run into problems, isn't it?
~Site Owner​

2014.07.12
Details about some of the listed bugs and other stuff

Hey guys, it's the site owner. And I've got something interesting to tell you today, covering a couple of the bugs with the level pack.

So first and utmost is the bug I've explained why it happens about 5 times already - the closed Hexagon glitch on Hexagonest, Hyper Hexagonest, Hyper Hexagonest's End and The End. While I was playing Apeirogon in VeeDefault and Fusion in Rotationality, I have actually noticed that this same glitch appears there. Unfortunately, that is Vee's hardpatterns.lua being misread by the game, which occurs if you do not have enough free RAM for the game to use up. This problem is also very apparent on an older machine (this game CHEWS on my old PC's CPU & RAM).

Next up is the problem with awesome.ogg not playing when needed. Now, the only level where this sound plays correctly in is Hyper Hexagonest. Other levels display the text for it, but do not play the sound. Why is this? Well, this is all due to the event limitation a certain level can have. The max amount of events 1 level can have is 15.       
{ "time": 180,     "type": "play_sound",     "id": "SHfOH_awesome.ogg" }, on most levels (except Hyper Hexagonest) is event #16, which the game does not read, due to it being over the limit. This is why awesome.ogg does not play in any other level than Hyper Hexagonest.

Then we have the music switching glitch. This bug is apparent to Hyper Hexagonest's End, when it has to switch to Beyond Hyper Hexagonest's End. The music for Hyper Hexagonest's End continues to play, even after the level has switched. Now, Baby Steps (the tutorial for Open Hexagon) does the level switching thing as well. However, the reason it's not getting any music glitches is because both levels use the exact same song, while Beyond Hyper Hexagonest's End uses an entirely different song from Hyper Hexagonest's End. Now, I did try to use commands like:
        { "time": 420,     "type": "music_stop" },
        { "time": 420,     "type": "music_set",     "id": "testlevelmusic" },
However, none of those commands worked. Since I couldn't find a fix for it online (really, no one makes guides for Open Hexagon), I just left it there. If anyone does know how to fix this, contact me ASAP and I'll include it in the next update (if there will be someone who will contact me anyway).

And then the 3D Effect: On glitch with the level pack. Why are the shadows under the polygons in most levels incorrect? Well, I never intended for this level pack to be played with the 3D Effect. I developed it fully with 3D Effect: Off, and never touched the 3D options in the styles. So if you don't want any style glitches, simply turn off the 3D effect and you should be all good. Well, maybe the levels could be tilted, but all you gotta do is restart the game.

And lastly - the music for the level continuing to play when you're in the menu.
I'll be honest - I genuinely have no idea why this happens. This happens to me (and to most people) on pretty much every level pack at random (it doesn't always happen. Sometimes it does, sometimes it doesn't). What causes it is unknown to me. But I did note that the glitch is rare with this level pack, so you shouldn't experience too often.

And that is about it, guys. Hope this post has been helpful, I'll try to keep you guys up-to-date.
Later.
~Site Owner​
