So...what happened to NFS Most Wanted Definitive Edition?

5/27/2026

 
A few months ago, I wrote about me making a modpack for Need for Speed: Most Wanted. The 2005 game, not the crappy 2012 one. I dubbed it "Definitive Edition". I even made a teaser video for it, showcasing a few of the mods I applied to the game in the following YouTube video.
But since then I've gone silent about it. Why? What happened?

The mod pack is no longer being worked on.

Simply put, after extensively testing it by going through the career mode of it 2 times - once on my Windows PC and once on my Steam Deck - and encountering issues I don't know how to account for or fix, I've decided to effectively shelve it. Below, I'll detail some of the issues I've encountered with the development of the mod pack.

Issue #1: Spinning Brakes & Unwanted Customization

After the initial installation of the NFSMW Unlimiter mod, a few things happened. Namely, brakes on cars started spinning if custom rims were applied. While a minor annoyance at first glance, I then realized that it's not something I could actually fix. I thought that installing the Leftover Brakes Fix would be what I would need to do, but that did not resolve anything. Just made the spinning brakes look prettier.

And despite during installation telling NFSMW Unlimiter that I do not have any additional customization mods installed, it still attempted to add & enable Front/Rear Headlight customization, tried adding Neons and a bunch of other stuff...which basically meant I had to start over from scratch.

Issue #2: Restoring Cut Vehicles

Specifically the BMW M3 E46. The base model. There are two mods that restore this car - one restores the model to make it actually functional & not a black BMW-shaped box. The other actually restores its performance & adds it to the Career Mode as a purchasable vehicle.

First of all, the customization on this car was lacking. No body kits. No working hood & roof scoop customization. The only things that did work were spoilers & wheels. Second - adding a vinyl to the car crashed the whole game. And if the game decided to spawn an M3 E46 in a race with a vinyl - yeah, that would lead to a random crash too.

I tried scouring NFSMods to see if there's anyone who made a different re-implementation of the base M3 E46, but I couldn't find anything. Plenty (and I'd figure too many) mods exist that expand what you can do with the M3 GTR, but the pool for the base M3 E46 is practically non-existent.

Although aside from the M3 E46 being a nightmare to re-implement, rather surprisingly - the other cut vehicles were not so difficult. Re-implementing the Nissan 350Z & Nissan Skyline GT-R R34 into the game - two known cut Nissan cars from the game - was rather easy and it was pretty painless. Adding the Black Edition exclusive cars into the Career Mode as purchasable vehicles to use throughout the campaign? Also pretty easy & not an issue. However...

Issue #3: Unlock Conditions

Re-implementing all these cut vehicles let to the side effect of the M3 GTR variants - Street, Prologue & Endgame - all being available for purchase at the start of the Career.

It's less of an issue than you would expect, granted, considering that the M3 GTR Street variant was set to be priced at 90,000 cash (whereas you start out with only 42,000 in the mod pack), and both the Prologue & Endgame M3 GTRs were set to be priced at 500,000 cash each, I'd ideally like to not have them be unlocked upon creating the save file. But I could not figure out how to change their unlock conditions after screwing around with NFS-VltEd.

My idea would've been that the M3 GTR Street variant would be unlocked after you beat Blacklist #8 Jewels, and the M3 GTR Endgame variant would be unlocked after you complete the game, much like how you get that car in your garage after clearing the final pursuit. The M3 GTR Prologue variant doesn't need to be there, as it's just the weaker duplicate of the Endgame M3 GTR. But, again - I couldn't figure out how to fix it.

Issue #4: Graphical glitches, plenty of crashes & softlocks

This is the most significant reason why I am no longer working on this mod pack. Mashing together so many mods has resulted in frequent graphical glitches, such as cars randomly becoming transparent, the game crashing upon trying to change any setting, and most recently - the game straight up freezing when trying to initiate a pursuit through the Bounty/Milestone screen. The only way to engage with the cops in the mod is to trigger them in free roam & finding them yourself. Which is just...no, I don't find that acceptable.

The graphical glitches are all likely part of the Xbox 360 Stuff pack that's been ported. My assumption is that when it's trying to deal with the add-ons that I've added to the game, it's likely pushing the engine to its limit, resulting in it trying to unload assets to save on memory. But I genuinely don't know.

In the end, what was the "Definitive Edition" meant to be?

In the end, what I wanted to do with this mod is make a version of NFS Most Wanted 2005 that included:
  • The PS2 Demo Content
  • The cars cut during the game's development - namely the M3 E46, 350Z and Skyline
  • Expanded & finished heat levels from 1 to 10
  • Graphics & cutscenes from the Xbox 360 version of the game
  • An uncensored soundtrack

All the while, trying to keep the original game's identity & period-accuracy fully in tact. The mod pack basically aimed to just make the vanilla NFS Most Wanted experience, an already good one, even better. And the reason I wanted to do it with this approach is because I'm just not satisfied with how many other mods with similar names end up doing non-sensical things, like adding Veyrons and other hypercars into the game that did not exist back in 2005.

But it just didn't end up working out. While mashing mods together to make your ideal version of the game sounds good on paper, it ultimately doesn't end up working out the way you would expect. And that's okay.

So...am I still on Linux?

3/23/2026

 
A few years back I wrote a post about how I had to leave Linux for the time being due to Nvidia's driver support being utterly awful. You can read all about that here.

However, since then, I've actually been back on Linux since around...August of 2024? Somewhere around that. At my (now former) job, one of my friends who I was working with at that job with mentioned how he would be interested in trading his RX 5700 XT for my RTX 3060 that I had at that time. I ended up taking that trade, actually.

Ever since then, I went back to Linux and have been on it ever since. This year, in a few months time, will mark 2 full years of me being on Linux. It's gotten to the point where I only ever use Windows these days for a few very specific things, such as modding old games where the tools don't really work on Linux. I have an entire secondary PC next to me with a Windows 11 install on it and it gets booted up like, at most, once a week.

So how's Linux been treating me over the last 2 years?

Honestly, ever since I made the graphics card switch from Nvidia to AMD, it's been...fantastic! I'm genuinely just shocked at how good AMD graphics cards work on Linux.

Granted, I did have to switch from that RX 5700 XT to an RX 7600 about 1 year into the trade. That reason being because the RX 5700 XT ended up kicking the bucket. But it wasn't because my former co-worker lied to me though, no. The graphics card was genuinely in good health when we did the trade. The reason the GPU died was namely due to how tiny my case is and I think I ended up suffocating it.

Hey, if anyone from Lithuania is reading this and they want a broken RX 5700 XT to tinker around with and try to revive it, let me know, could arrange something I guess.

The RX 7600 I ended up getting fits into my case much nicer, has a lot more breathing room and it has been a treat ever since I got it.

But the Linux experience has been honestly great. I've gotten to the point now where my Fedora install now does absolutely everything, and I mean everything, that I need it to do. Everything to the last minor detail now just works as if I were on Windows.

I've experimented with a few desktop environments, namely GNOME, XFCE and Cinnamon, but I honestly still find KDE to be the best one for my personal needs simply because it requires re-learning things a lot less than other desktop environments.

I've also dabbled around with other distros, namely Arch, Linux Mint and Ubuntu, but I still personally find Fedora to be the one I'm most comfortable with. So I've stuck with Fedora.

I've run some more fun things on Fedora, you can check a video down below where I ran TUFU (2Unreal5Underground) on my Fedora install. Still using that same install, by the way! Only difference is that I'm currently on Fedora 43 now.

A few snags on my journey

When Fedora 43 was released, myself and a lot (and I mean a lot) of other people started experiencing issues upgrading from Fedora 42 to 43. The reason? Wine, for some reason. Here's my Reddit post about it when it was relevant. I had to delete Wine & reinstall it after the upgrade to 43. I did have to fix a few Wine prefixes, mainly osu-winello, but that ended up being far easier than I anticipated.

(note: even though I said "for some reason", the actual reason being that 32-bit WIne has been deprecated and is no longer supported, so that was the reason the upgrades were being blocked)

One thing I never ended up getting working on Fedora was, sadly, my Hauppauge HD PVR 2 capture card. I ended up giving up on it and put it away. Thank you crafciak31 for the HD PVR 2 that you gifted me ages ago at this point, I still really appreciate it as a sick as hell donation, but now the thing is currently in a box, piled on top of other misc. tech that I have but don't use.

DaVinci Resolve...never got it to be stable on Fedora, sadly. I could not figure out how to make it stable and not crash every time I tried to do a timeline change. I ended up giving up on that as well. I heard there are video editors that work fine on Linux though, so I'll have to look into those when I feel bothered to. Not right now though, it's not a priority at all for me at the current moment.

Steam Deck!

I actually got a Steam Deck in October 2025! And I've been enjoying my time using it so far. I don't have much to say about it, other than I think it's a really cool piece of tech.

My Steam Deck ended up replacing my Lenovo Thinkpad E490, which I recently donated to a friend of mine. I'll miss that laptop but I'm also much happier knowing that it's off to a new home where it'll continue to be used.

Would I recommend Linux to an average user though?

Honestly...still no. Despite how many advancements Linux has made, I still don't think Linux is at a point where an average user could just jump into it and have everything work. It's still very much a platform for nerds who like tinkering around to get stuff work.

Granted, I don't think Linux is that hard, it really isn't. It's just time-consuming. Anything and everything you would want to do is one simple internet search away from figuring out, but the problem is that it just takes a lot of time, especially if you're new.

Sure, setting up a new web browser and surfing the net is easy enough. Most Linux distros come with Firefox packaged in, most Chromium-based browsers work fine on Linux too, including the crime to humanity known as Google Chrome. Getting Steam to run and getting your Steam Library to work out of the box is also super simple and super easy. I get that. Even non-Steam games that utilize Wine are also not that much of a hassle to get going, especially if you use something like Lutris.

But the main thing is that the moment you decide to step away from doing anything that isn't already working out of the box, figuring that stuff out genuinely takes more time than it's worth for some people. Linux still has ways to go in making things just work. That's where I think Windows still has an advantage over Linux. It's in software support. All you need to do on Windows is download an installer, install it and off you go.

On Linux, I wish it were that easy with some stuff. I really do.

With Linux, it's still a case of where I have to ask my friends "well, what exactly do you want to do on your PC?" when it comes to recommending it to them. For people like artists, music producers and such, as I do have a few friends who do that kind of creative work, I still cannot recommend Linux to them. One for a lack of experience doing that sort of stuff so I don't know whether they could get their software to reliably work on Linux, the other...is hardware. Nvidia is still, to this day, the biggest roadblock for me recommending Linux to others, purely based off my own experience. And a majority of my friends have Nvidia GPUs. I earnestly still cannot recommend Linux to anyone with an Nvidia GPU.

If all you need out of your PC is a web browser, Discord and Steam for all your games, then yeah, Linux is a good fit for you. But if you want to do anything beyond that basic set of stuff, then no. I don't think I can earnestly recommend Linux to you.

What's next?

Well, I don't plan on leaving Linux anytime soon. It's been treating me very, very well and I have no complaints. I've been slowly but surely de-Windows'ing my life, ever since I made that decision in June 2024 to leave Windows behind due to Microsoft's shift to making Windows an "agentic OS" with all of the unnecessary AI crap no one asked for and no one is using, on top of being an insane privacy risk. I couldn't stand Windows becoming even more like spyware than it used to be.

As for my next post...I promised something relating to Windows XP and I still intend to make a blog post about a particular piece of hardware I received. And that's still in my plans! On top of writing about a mod pack for Need for Speed Most Wanted (2005) that I'm working on.

Safe to say, I have things I want to talk about. But until I find the motivation to do so, this little blog post is what you're getting for now.

Thanks for reading!

Lots of things have happened

3/1/2026

 
I really don't know how to formulate my thoughts. In October 2024, I wanted to originally leave Weebly and migrate elsewhere. I was ready for it. I had even started to remake pages on the new web host as well. But then...life happened. I need a space where I can really just...talk. Then I remembered - that's right, I have a website that is literally my own personal space.

End of 2024

To say that I had a...turbulent ending to 2024 would be a huge understatement. I don't think there's anything that could've really prepared me for what was going to happen at the end of that year. I had just come back from my first ever solo trip abroad, going to cavoe's osu! event (COE) that year. But the timing was less than fortunate. The event had landed in the week that my mom's birthday was on, which, yeah, she wasn't entirely happy about. But I had already planned everything out - booked the flights, booked the hotel, got my convention ticket. And furthermore, I really didn't want to travel together with my mom to an osu! event.

It's one thing to leave your family member at home but it's another to leave them alone in a country where she doesn't speak the native language or know English that well. I didn't want to deal with that additional headache on top of things. So when it was actually her birthday on July 30th, I called her. Least I could do is say happy birthday, even if I am over 1000 kilometers away. But she was completely drunk. Crying and screaming at me so loud that other people in the area next to me could hear her, even if I didn't have her on loudspeaker. I just remember hanging up on her and not picking the phone up again. My other friends I was traveling with were shocked too, they knew that my mom had a tendency of this but this was the first time they got to witness it first-hand in real life.

After I came back home, my mom started to drink. A lot. And I really mean...a lot. So much so that she tried to hide it by placating me with snacks and drinks when I came back home from work. Some people asked me why I never moved out, I was wondering that too but then remembered that moving out wouldn't be so easy given current wages. I had been window-shopping for an apartment to rent for some time by then but couldn't find anything. So I kinda toughed it out, guess it helped me save some money not having to rent my own place too.

In November, my mom went on her own solo-trip, this time to the UK to visit my brother and his family. And I was left alone for 3 weeks. All I can remember from that time is how...quiet everything was without my mom around. Every day I would come home after work and it'd be...quiet. Usually I'd find her in her room watching some TV shows on her PC but when she was away for 3 weeks, it was...odd how quiet it had gotten. It was during this time that I had actually started to sit down and start making some marginal progress on my website move that I had planned.

Then when she returned from her trip, things were off. The previous year in 2023 when she went to the UK on her own, I remember how absolutely star-struck she was about my brother's kids, especially his newborn one at the time. But this year? She came back...bitter. Apparently having gotten into a fight during the trip with my brother and his wife. I didn't pry too much but I did ask my brother what was up. Why not ask my mom at the time? I did. She didn't want to talk about it. My brother mentioned that my mom kept asking to be let go solo to go to places like London and Manchester, meanwhile my brother was flat broke and also didn't want my mom wandering around the UK while she spoke literally zero English. I understood my brother's POV but my mom saw this as being confined intentionally.

It was a week after this trip that I came home from work and I saw that my mom was fully drunk. Like, fully shit-faced drunk. And she started harassing me. Asking me to clean up her pissed up mattress. Asking me to help her with her computer issues. Asking me to go to the store to get her more booze. Keep in mind it was late in the evening, early December and incredibly cold outside. I was insanely tired after work and didn't want to do anything, my mom started calling me a lazy slob. I...couldn't take it anymore. Lashed out at her. First, a shout-match ensued between the both of us. But then my mom started physically hitting me. With her first, with random objects she found, she genuinely tried to hurt me.

It was at that moment that I had decided enough was enough. I packed as many things as I could carry, grabbed my e-Scooter and went out. It was -6°C outside, and here I was with 4 bags and an e-Scooter, looking for a place to stay. I asked around as many friends as I could if anyone would be willing to let me crash for the weekend. Thankfully one friend said yes.

During that weekend, I heard nothing from my mom. Zilch. Sunday evening I called her from my work phone, since I didn't want her knowing it's me calling. She picked up, that was enough. I went home that evening, found her fully passed out drunk. I just took a shower and went to sleep myself. Early the next morning I started texting around, looking for someplace to move to. Eventually one of my former work colleagues from my previous job told me they had a vacant room in the apartment they were living at, asked if I can bring my cat, asked what the rent price is and by Tuesday, I had started actually moving my stuff to the new apartment. That was when my mom started to actually begin beg, cry & ask me not to leave her. But I've been through that song & dance many times over the last few years, I couldn't take it anymore. So I told her that this is the consequence of her own actions. By Saturday, I had finished moving and started living separately from my mom for the first time...ever.

We barely kept in touch. I flat out told her that if she wants to have any kind of positive relations with me, she needs to start taking her alcoholism problem seriously. I told her that if I catch her drunk one more time, I'd be cutting contact and then gave her suggestions on what to do next, being psychological and psychiatric help. She promised she would, I left it at that.

Fast forward to Christmas 2024. I decided I want to go visit mom for the holidays. That day I remember calling her and getting no reply, which was very odd. I made my way to what was now my former home and I tried unlocking the door. I can't. My mom had locked herself into the apartment with no way of getting in. I start calling her and after like 5 attempts, she picked up. I asked her to let me in, she said okay. 10 minutes later, she unlocked the door and I was able to enter the apartment.

First things I noticed - the place is a complete and total mess. Unwashed dishes. Garbage bags everywhere. And the undeniable smell of straight up urine. And my mom? She looked...disheveled. Messy hair. Wet/greasy clothes. Not even wearing underwear? Yeah, what the fuck. Told her to put some pants on, sat down next to her and I wanted to just talk to her. I abandoned my original plan of inviting her out to the city to explore the Christmas market together because something was clearly up. But my mom, she was not listening. All I could get out of her was a groggy "WHAT?!" before she would proceed to pretend that I don't exist in the room at all, watching Russian TV shows at volumes that would make anyone's ears hurt.

At that point, I have had enough. I straight up told her "This is it. This is what you're choosing, huh? Well, consider yourself gone from my life." and I went to pack whatever loose things I still had left behind. Things like slippers, some old consoles I thought I'd maybe want to play down the line. While I was packing though, my mom actually stood up and angrily opened my room's door and screamed at me to not cut her out of my life. I just repeated to her that I told her this would happen and these are just the consequences of her actions, and she went back to her room.

After I grabbed some extra stuff, I just left the apartment, finished packing what I couldn't fit outside of it because I just could not stand the smell anymore. While I was outside, I actually heard her lock herself into the apartment again. In hindsight, I should've gone back. But at that point I was completely done and didn't want to speak to my mom anymore, ever again.

Now the horrible part.

5 days later, on December 30th, 2024, my brother started calling me in the middle of work. I went to a private call box and picked up, asked him what's up. I thought he was just calling me to wish me well for the upcoming new year but...it was something else.

He had mentioned that he hasn't been able to get in contact with mom since Christmas. He already knew what happened, since I did confide in him moments after it happened on Christmas day, so he knew I was no longer on speaking terms with her. But he pleaded with me to actually go and check on her, just to see if she's okay. He said I don't even have to talk to her, just make sure that she's okay and that's all he needs. I agreed, told my boss the situation and he was instantly understanding of the situation and told me to just take the rest of the day off to sort this mess out.

First I had to go to the new apartment I was renting, since the keys to my old place were in the drawer. Grabbed the keys and then went back immediately to mom's place. Yep, locked herself in again, exactly the same as Christmas. I start knocking on the door - no answer. I knocked for what felt like ages, but no one came to the door. Then I began pretty much spam-calling her. Over, and over, and over, and over, and over...no reply. I was at a loss. I had been standing there for over an hour, banging on the door and calling her to...nothing. I started panicking. I called my brother, told him the situation and he advised I call emergency services. This does not look good at all.

Hands shaking, I dial 112 and explain the situation. Not even 5 minutes later, two firetrucks, an ambulance and a police car show up. The firetruck had an extendable ladder, and thankfully my mom left the balcony completely open, so the firemen just had to get in through the balcony and they were able to get the keys out and unlock the apartment.

I wasn't allowed inside initially. There were safety things they had to check, as well as check up on mom. But that's the thing. After 10 minutes, they let me in with the news...mom's dead. Mom had...died. She was found dead on the floor of the apartment.

Everything just...shattered for me. Words genuinely cannot express how...soul-crushing that was. My mom was...dead. And I felt responsible for her death. I don't know if she died on the 25th, 26th or any other date, or if when we found her was the day that she died. But it also didn't matter. My mom was dead.

2025 - a rough start to the year

2025 was a hellish year for me. The year started off with mom's funeral. Which was entirely funded by my friends & my mom's friends donating to the funeral. But to think I'd start the year with...burying my mom...was...well, I can't put it into words.

The following months were mentally some of the hardest I've had to endure. I was incredibly desperate for any kind of companionship from...anyone. I have no family left in the country that I live in. I have no father, no grandparents, no cousins, nothing. I have nothing, I have no one. I genuinely felt alone and isolated. Like the universe taking my mom away from me being a gigantic middle finger. Me getting what I deserved. That's what I believed too.

I've never felt more alone in my life during this time period. It eventually culminated into me having yet another attempt on my life. For those not in the know, my birthday is February 24th. What did I decide to do on February 23rd, 2025 - one day before my 26th birthday? I decided that I would be committing suicide because I had also lost my online best friend at the time. One of the people I went on the trip to COE 2024 with. Someone I had known since 2016. I couldn't bare it anymore. We had gotten into a fight over Counter-Strike 2 and they ended up removing me everywhere - Discord, Steam, osu! - anywhere and everywhere we talked.

That was genuinely the straw that broke the camel's back for me. That was it. I had lost everything. I don't HAVE anything else to lose anymore. I don't have any family, and now it seems like I don't have any friends left either. The ones that were left just felt...fake. They didn't feel like actual friends whatsoever. So I decided - fuck it. My life isn't worth anything, might as well just end it.

Thankfully, I am an idiot and publicly posted about this to my private Instagram filled with IRL people who know exactly where I live. But in my head, none of them gave a single shit what would happen to me, so the least I could tell them is that I'm going to be gone for good and not be in anyone's hair anymore. That ended up saving my life as several of my IRL friends immediately called the police. Before I had the chance to do anything to myself, not 20 minutes later - police were knocking at my door, alongside one of my IRL friends who saw the Instagram posts.

The police started interrogating me on what the hell was I trying to do to myself. I told them, without bullshitting them, that I was just insanely depressed and suicidal after feeling like I've been left completely alone. I lost my family. My friends felt fake, all of that. After the interrogation, they called medics over and I told the same thing to them. At that point, they told me to pack some stuff, grab my passport and put me into an ambulance. I was driven to the psych ward. I was hospitalized.

But you know what? That hospital stay genuinely wasn't bad...at all. There, I met people who were very similar to me. Down on their luck. People who understood me right off the gate. I didn't have to tell them anything in any kind of roundabout way or anything to avoid unwanted "advice" like "oh life gets better everyone has these moments" type of things. No, these people got me instantly. And they spoke my language, if you get what that means. And that hospital stay genuinely helped me mentally get back on my feet because I realized that people who do understand me exist out there. I kept in touch with some of them after being released from the hospital but, as these things go, we all basically went our separate ways after I or they were discharged. I haven't really heard much from them.

It was at this point that I felt like things started looking up. I wasn't doing great. My hospital stay really highlighted and emphasized how fake everyone around me felt. Going from a place where people embraced and encouraged me to be...me back to a place where I had to fake being nice was...exhausting. But for the first time in my life, I felt as though I finally had people wanting to help me. I was signed up for monthly psychiatrist checks and slowly but surely started going to therapy again. I hadn't done that since 2022. My psychiatrist eventually signed me up for a 6-week group therapy program at the clinic I go to. I was very hesitant about accepting it at first (it was offered to me back in March when I was initially discharged from the hospital) but ended up accepting it.

One Week With Linux

6/27/2024

 
So, it's been a week since I've switched to Linux now. A bit over a week now, actually. I've had time to acclimate & get used to doing things a bit "differently" than what I was used to with Windows, but overall - I'd say I've gotten to the point where I no longer really feel the difference between how I did things with Windows & how I do things with Linux.

Then again, what I wrote in the previous post was probably too bloated & emotional anyway. Without further ado, let's get into how the Linux adventure has been for me so far.

Switching out of Wayland

A majority of the issues outlined in my previous post, such as the clipboard not working & Firefox refusing to co-operate were actually the result of Wayland, believe it or not.

I've heard many warnings from all walks of life that Nvidia GPUs & Linux don't really tend to mix together. While the Linux kernel has an open-source driver for it called Nouveau & Nvidia technically does provide drivers for Linux, I've heard from many people all across the web that Nvidia GPUs don't tend to mesh together with Linux all too well. It's why most people just recommend using AMD instead because AMD open-sources their drivers & lets people fix any issues that arise when using Linux.

I don't have the luxury of just swapping out my RTX 3060 for a different GPU though so I'm stuck with what I have. The way I installed KDE Plasma when I first installed Fedora was just installing the default KDE Plasma package that you get when you type in the terminal command for it, and KDE Plasma (to my understanding) comes with Wayland as the default.

Wayland sucks on Nvidia. Yes, my clipboard & Firefox woes were caused by it but I've also experienced many graphical freakouts on the way as well. I'll show what I'm talking about in a later section of this post but yeah, the experience wasn't pleasant.

A guy from Mastodon then told me to try installing KDE Plasma with X11 instead & try using that, gave me the terminal command to install it. Tried it & all of a sudden - every major issue I've had with Linux vanished almost simultaneously...but a few others cropped up along the way.

X11 didn't start out smooth

When I first installed KDE Plasma X11, the first thing that I immediately noticed was that my mouse felt...off. It's then that I realized that mouse acceleration was permanently enabled, despite the fact that I unchecked the setting in System Settings for it.

I can't stand mouse acceleration. It messes with my aim in FPS games & osu!, and generally just doesn't make for a great user experience for me. The mouse feels imprecise with it & often feel like I'm fighting with it more than I'm using the computer.

So, with some guidance from folks in various Discord servers knowledgeable about this kind of stuff, what I ended up doing is I created a file in /etc/X11/xorg.conf.d/ called 40-libinput.conf and pasted the following:

 Section "InputClass"
  Identifier "libinput pointer catchall"
  MatchIsPointer "on"
  MatchDevicePath "/dev/input/event*"
  Driver "libinput"
  Option "AccelProfile" "flat"
 EndSection

After making the file, pasting the above in there & rebooting - my mouse was fixed! Felt usable, finally.

But there was another issue. Sometimes, for an inexplicable reason, one of my monitors would just fail to start up. I'm running a 3 monitor setup, 2 of which are running at 60hz and my primary one is a 144hz panel. The 144hz monitor would usually be the one that would fail to initialize and I'd be stuck with 2 monitors, but it's not consistent. Sometimes one of the 60hz monitors would fail to start up in the same way.

Unfortunately I didn't find the reason for this happening but often times a system reboot would just fix the issue so I don't see it as that big of a deal.

Though despite the issues I've come across thus far, I'd still say that it was worth the switch from Wayland to X11 in my specific use-case, since I'd rather just have games not start up with garbled graphics & for my clipboard to work as it should...it even fixed unplayable flickering in osu!lazer & somehow fixed Vulkan failing to initialize on it!

osu! (stable) on Linux was more work than I expected

One of the early goals I had set out for myself was not just to get osu!lazer up & running, but to also get my Windows backup of my osu! (stable) install going as well. Truthfully I believed that the process would be as simple as just installing Wine & running the osu!.exe file under Wine. That train of thought didn't get me very far.

So I got some help from a guy called Ravener, who came across my Linux-related posts on Mastodon. They actually started out with the osu!lazer flickering issues I mentioned above & helped me solve it under Wayland, as well as gave me a genuinely useful resource to use called "Gear Lever" for AppImage management.

Anyway, the steps I had to go through to get osu! (stable) working on Linux were as follows:
  • Set up osu-winello
  • Realize I am an idiot and do not know how hidden folders on Linux work (CTRL+H btw)
  • Install/update winetricks
  • Configure fonts & fail at it (for JP/KR/CHS/CHT/etc. text to show up properly)

I switched from Windows to Linux

6/19/2024

 
Yesterday, June 18th 2024, I decided to finally make a big leap, one that I was contemplating for a very long time. I finally swapped from Windows to Linux.

But why? What distro did I end up going with? What's my history with Linux anyway? I want to make this blog post pretty much to talk about why I switched from Windows, how I even got introduced to Linux to begin with, and then discuss the personal hell I've walked through ever since I made the switch.

My history with Linux

Truth be told, Linux is something I've had an interest in since 2013. Back when Steam first added support for Linux. Up until that point, I had never heard of Linux. I didn't know what it was, nor how to use it. My friend had invited me over to his house to try to help him set it up, so he could get the Tux item in Team Fortress 2, which was a promotional item distributed for about 2 weeks to players on Linux.

Back then, we managed to figure out how to dual-boot Windows 7 & Ubuntu together, and after an entire day's of effort - we managed to get Ubuntu, Steam & Team Fortress 2 installed. It felt like a herculean effort but we managed it & my friend still has the Tux item in TF2 to commemorate that. I didn't get it though, sadly. One of the biggest things I regret missing out on in TF2 during my time. And the fact Valve never made the item tradable despite promising to really, really irked me to no end, but whatever. It's been 11 years since then, I've made my peace with it.

But that day in 2013 lit a spark that never really faded away. I took a genuine interest in Linux. I was already skeptical of the direction Microsoft was headed towards with Windows even back then. Windows 8 failed to impress with its tablet-esque UI that just sorta ended up driving me away from it all. When Windows 10 was announced & then released in 2015, I tried it out day 1...and hated it. But I'm willing to keel over in the sense that I don't think my issues with Windows 10 were as bad as I mentioned them to be in my original video. I think it was more-so the fact that, unknowingly, my laptop at that time was just dying. You could even see it in some of my other videos too where the graphics chip in it was starting to beg for its life, an example I keep pointing to is this TF2 video I made back in 2015. Just look at the framerate absolutely tanking & the graphics glitching out on a constant basis. I thought it was just one of the many mods that I installed that caused the game to glitch out like that, but I'm way more willing to believe that it was because of the graphics chip being almost literally on fire.

Over the years after, while I continued to stick to Windows, as I have been using it my whole life, my interest in Linux never faded. I was always hesitant to make the switch because I was just afraid of how would I manage doing anything - video editing, gaming, livestreaming - all of that. These were factors that kept me away from ever jumping from Windows to Linux full-time.

The way I evaluated the Linux experience over the years was simple. I just tried it. When I upgraded my PC over the years, my old ones, especially my laptops (fun fact - I full-timed laptops from 2010 to 2016!) were just kinda there sitting around, not really doing much. So, since I wasn't using them anymore, I've used them as my testing beds for Linux.

Most distros I've tried over the years were Debian-based:
  • Ubuntu
  • Lubuntu
  • Linux Mint

These were the three main distros I used to go with. Ubuntu is obvious - it's pretty much everyone's first distro of choice. Lubuntu though, I tried it on an old Pentium M laptop I had, which was the Windows XP laptop I used to use as my main PC from 2010 to 2014. Only reason I ever gave it a shot was because it was advertised as a very lightweight distro for older computers, and considering that Pentium M laptop only ever had 1GB of RAM & Intel 965 GMA graphics, I thought Lubuntu fit the bill on it nicely. And it was! Lubuntu ran pretty nicely on it back in late 2016 until the hard drive in the laptop finally died & refused to boot ever again. I ended up selling the laptop the next year for 5 euros, since it was old & in very, very poor condition.

I have a video of that laptop running Lubuntu on my YouTube channel, but that video was later made private. Mainly because I didn't know what the hell I was talking about, it was filmed on a phone camera & trust me, the laptop really was that disgusting. It's a video I'll never put back up for public view again, but you can at least have the title & thumbnail of it down below. That's about as much publicity I'm ever willing to give that video again.
Yeesh, that description really is....something. Remember back when I used Twitter? I'd say those were the "good old days" or something but I'd definitely be lying.

The point is, I'm no stranger to Linux. I've known about it since early 2013 and have been keeping tabs on it every now and then.

My most recent experiences with Linux were, surprisingly enough, with Linux Mint. It's a distro I've used consistently on my old college laptop that my brother gave me back in 2018 as a present for managing to enroll into college, constantly flip-flopping between Windows 10 & Linux Mint, mainly because Windows 10 on that laptop ran horribly. Funnily enough, I never dual-booted Windows and Linux on it. I always had one or the other on it. Why switch from Windows?

My reasons for switching out of Windows are pretty simple, albeit might be seen as petty by some. I'm just not happy with the direction Microsoft is headed towards with Windows. Again. Same thing I said about Windows 8, now the same thing is being said by me about Windows 11.

First reason - I'm not a fan of the fact that Microsoft is killing off older hardware with Windows 11's system requirements. This move by Microsoft is the reason why I even got a new PC in 2022 at all, actually. Because the threat of not being able to use Windows anymore past Windows 10 really fucking sucked. And it still sucks, even now.

My old i7-4790k PC can still kick ass. It can still do things. I know this, because I still used it up until recently. Everything I threw at it, it handled everything flawlessly. Be it video encoding, gaming, video editing, even being a damn server - it can hold its own still. Yet Microsoft deems it "too old" for Windows 11, all because of TPM 2.0 not being supported on it.

Listen, I know that there are still ways of getting Windows 11 installed on "unsupported hardware" but that's not my point. My point is that Microsoft is pretty much just forcing people to ditch their perfectly working hardware arbitrarily. The TPM requirement for Windows 11 is very arbitrary and what sucks is that Microsoft doesn't give you an alternative outside of "get a new PC loser".

Second reason - artificial intelligence. I've made a blog post about it here and even reiterate constantly that nothing on this website is AI generated. With all of that, you can probably guess that I'm just not a big fan of AI. I personally think it's a very dangerous tool that has been used, so far, for more harm than it has been used for good.

Now we have AI-generated voice models, which have already been used for criminal activity to impersonate other people, and even AI-generated videos are now starting to be a thing.

Just...no. I don't like this whole AI shtick, and I really wish they did what NFTs did and just fucked off back into irrelevance but that doesn't seem to be the way the industry is headed. Everything is just becoming more focused on AI.

And Microsoft now incorporating AI into Windows itself is where I draw the line. Windows 11 24H2 looks to be like it's just going to be a shitshow. And these two reasons were enough to be the straw that broke the camel's back for me. These two reasons are why I finally decided to make the big leap from Windows to Linux.

How has the Linux experience been so far? A little bit...hell-ish, not gonna lie.

A lot of that has to do with my lack of experience in using Linux as a whole beyond the surface level. If you recall, I've been a Windows user my whole life. Counter-Strike 2 and Team Fortress 2 (#FixTF2 btw) I have nothing to comment on. Because these games are native on Linux, I haven't had any issues with them at all. This story begins with the fact that my friend & I both noticed that my two internal SATA drives were being shown as "Removable Media" on Dolphin. My friend then suggested I edit the fstab file & put in entries for both of my drives so that they end up being mounted permanently & I wouldn't need to manually mount them every single boot. The way we mounted my drives was by first figuring out their identifiers, a.k.a. /dev/sda1 and /dev/sdb1, which are the identifiers for my HDD & SSD respectively. After that, we edited the fstab file by adding these lines into them:
  • /dev/sda1 /mnt/toshiba ntfs defaults 0 0
  • /dev/sdb1 /mnt/gamessd ntfs defaults 0 0
Reboot the system and voila - both drives were now permanently mounted. "That's that" I thought and proceeded to install Apex Legends onto the SSD, only for the game to not work.
